A bug-fix release: playback that could get stuck or start by itself after quick presses, and how a
lone letter "a" is spoken. Found by a review of the whole code and checked by driving the
application with real speech.

## Fixed

- **Pressing Play straight after changing the speed no longer leaves the reader silent.** Reading
  could stay on "Buffering" with no sound until Play was pressed again.
- **Stop, Next or Previous pressed while a passage is about to start now wins.** The passage
  being started could still begin playing afterwards, or the status could say it was playing over
  silence.
- **Editing a passage's words, or asking for a new reading of its maths, while reading is
  starting no longer leaves the reader on "Preparing speech".** Reading now starts in the new
  words.
- **Choosing a voice after a passage failed to speak no longer starts reading by itself.**
- **Opening one document straight after another** no longer lets the first one's status show over
  the second's.
- **A speed change just as one passage gives way to the next** no longer repeats the start of the
  next passage.
- **"a" in prose and in maths is told apart more reliably.** "averaged over a window", "given a
  sequence", "plus a constant" and "(A similar result holds.)" are read with the article, and
  "Let a denote", "a(t)", "a′", "from a to b" and "the point a lies" with the letter. Passages are
  spoken again once, as the words given to the speech engine have changed.
- **A preference that could not be saved when the application closed** (a read-only or full disk)
  is now said at the next start, instead of being lost without a word.

checksums.

## Known limitations

As before: English only; the Windows builds have not yet been run on a Windows PC; the optional
forced aligner is Linux-only and installed from source; pages are shown one at a time. A rotated
scanned page can still have a phrase read twice where two sideways lines meet. "the point a (the
origin) lies" is still read with the article, and "(i) A model" with the letter.
