A bug-fix release. A review of the whole program found fifteen bugs, and testing the fixes in the
running application found one more. Each one is fixed here.

## Fixed

### Playback

- **A pause between two passages is kept.** Pressing Pause just as one passage ended, or while
  the next was still being prepared, showed "Paused", and then the next passage played anyway.
  Now it waits for Play.
- **A corrected passage is read again in its new words.** Correcting or regenerating the passage
  being heard left its old audio playing, at first without a highlight and then with the new
  words' highlighting over it. It now starts again from the beginning, in the new wording.
- **A regeneration the model refuses keeps your place.** If the model could not be asked, the
  old wording was kept, but a paused passage went back to its first word.
- **Regenerate asks the model for a sentence and the equation it introduces.** For that kind of
  passage, Regenerate prepared the whole document again without asking the model, and lost your
  place.
- **The next passage is never heard in wording that has just been corrected.** A correction made
  while the next passage was being queued could still let the old version play.
- **A sound device that cannot start is reported.** If the output had gone away (a Bluetooth
  headset switched off, say), Play showed the reading as playing, with no sound and no error.

### What is read

- **Intervals and dates are no longer left out with citations.** With citations off (the
  default), "values in [0, 1]" was read as "values in", and dated asides such as "(from January
  1990 to March 2015)" and "the Great Depression (1929)" were dropped. A bracket holding a 0 or
  following a word such as "interval", "on" or "∈" is now kept, and month names and names after
  "the" are no longer taken for authors.
- **"Restore as prose" works on equations spread over several lines.** Until now it had no
  effect on them.
- **Spoken maths no longer contains "+", "/", "*" or "f(x)".** A speech engine reads those as
  symbols; such an answer is now refused and asked for again. (Cached maths readings are made
  again once.)
- **A count or a time that the paper itself gives** ("over 100 samples", "the first 5 seconds")
  is no longer refused in a maths reading.

### Files, keys and windows

- **An audiobook can no longer be written over the PDF.** `rail-narrator export --m4b paper.pdf`
  took the PDF's name as the audiobook's and replaced the PDF with audio. Options and the
  document are now read apart, and a path equal to the document is refused.
- **A maths endpoint's error message is never shown or saved.** When an endpoint refused a
  request, up to 300 characters of its reply went into the passage's warning, which is shown in
  Maths review and kept in a saved narration, and the reply can echo part of an API key. Warnings
  now give only the status and the server's name, and each reason once rather than once per
  retry.
- **Ctrl+Left and Ctrl+Right move by a word in a text box.** In the maths correction box and the
  note editor they jumped to another passage and started reading.
- **Closing a document lets go of its saved narration.** On Windows, saving a narration again for
  a document opened from one failed at the very end, after all the speech had been made.
- **Stopping the ZONOS3 server no longer waits five seconds, and cannot crash the application.**

Because what is read has changed, documents are prepared again the first time they are opened,
and their speech is made again.

| **Linux, AppImage** | `rail-narrator-x86_64.AppImage`: make it executable and run it. [AppImageUpdate](https://github.com/AppImageCommunity/AppImageUpdate) can update an earlier AppImage to this one. |
| **Linux, archive** | `rail-narrator-0.1.7-linux-x64.tar.gz`. Needs PortAudio (`libportaudio2` on Debian and Ubuntu, `portaudio` on Fedora). |

Both Linux downloads need glibc 2.35 or newer (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 and later). The
Windows downloads are built and tested automatically but have **not yet been run on a Windows PC**,
and are not code-signed (SmartScreen: *More info* ▸ *Run anyway*). `SHA256SUMS.txt` has the
checksums.

## Known limitations

As before: English only; the Windows builds have not yet been run on a Windows PC; the optional
forced aligner is Linux-only and installed from source; pages are shown one at a time. With
citations off, "values in [1, 5]" is still read as a citation and left out, since "in [n]" is
also how citations are written.
